01 — Foundations

What is the XRPL?

The XRP Ledger is a public blockchain that keeps a shared record of accounts, balances, and transfers. Anyone can read it. No single company has to approve each payment.

A shared notebook for value

Think of the ledger as a notebook copied across many independent computers. When you send value, those computers agree on the next page. Once validated, the result is final.

Why it exists

Moving money across borders is still slow and expensive. The XRPL was designed as a settlement layer: fast, inexpensive, and always-on.

02 — The machinery

How the technology works

Consensus, validators, and the UNL

Independent validators propose the next set of transactions and vote in short rounds. Each server listens to a Unique Node List. When a supermajority agrees, that ledger is final.

Accounts, balances, and transactions

An account holds XRP and, if you choose, other tokens. A transaction is a signed instruction. The fee is tiny and is destroyed, which helps deter spam.

Issued currencies

XRP is native. Other assets are issued by an account. Two tokens with the same name from different issuers are not the same asset.

Trust lines

Before you can hold an issued token, you usually open a trust line. Always check the issuer address, not just the ticker.

The native DEX

The ledger has had a built-in exchange since 2012. You place an offer and the network matches it.

Escrow

Lock value until a time or a condition is met.

Payment channels

Send many tiny claims off-ledger, then settle the net result on-chain.

NFTs

Native unique tokens (XLS-20) without a custom contract per collection.

AMM

A two-asset pool that quotes a price from the mix of assets inside it.

03 — Culture

The Riddler’s World

The ledger settles in seconds. The culture learned to wait in years.

Around the XRP Ledger, folklore became a place people call the Riddler’s World: long-term holders, visual puzzles, and a shared language that is not part of the protocol. Treat the lore as culture, not confirmation.

The number 589

A community symbol of conviction. It is not a protocol constant, not a Ripple target, and not a promise.

Riddles and holders

The Bearableguy tradition mixed finance, history, and humor. The deeper story is social: people who learned to read slowly and stay with the plumbing.

Culture meets code

Curiosity about a riddle can become curiosity about consensus. That crossover is the healthy version of the lore.

04 — In the world

Key advantages and real use cases

Cross-border payments

A payment that used to hop through correspondent banks can settle as an on-ledger transfer in seconds.

Tokenization

Stablecoins and other issued assets can live as trust-line tokens. Holders still need to trust the issuer.

On-chain trading

The native DEX and AMM let people trade XRP and issued tokens without a centralized order book.

05 — First steps

Getting started safely

1

Learn the words first

Account, reserve, trust line, issuer, offer.

2

Start with official docs

Use xrpl.org as your base layer.

3

Practice with a tiny amount

Create a wallet, fund it with a small sum, send a test payment.

4

Write the recovery phrase on paper

Never screenshot it. Anyone with those words can empty the account.

5

Verify issuer addresses

Match the issuer from a primary source before you open a trust line.

6

Prefer self-custody you understand

Exchanges are convenient and are also someone else’s computer.

7

Ignore urgency

No support agent or riddle account needs your seed.

8

Leave room for the reserve

Keep extra XRP above the network reserve so you can still send later.

Educational only. Not financial advice. On-ledger transfers are usually irreversible.
